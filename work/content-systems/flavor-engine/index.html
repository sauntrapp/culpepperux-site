Generative feature · Agent behavior · Risk

>The Flavor Engine: designing, stress-testing and retiring a generative feature.

A generative feature that worked well produced one output that could have created legal exposure. I designed layered mitigations, then recognized that the mechanism itself couldn't be made safe, and rebuilt the agent's job around what it could truthfully say.

Product
Sauntr, a swipe-based local venue discovery app (iOS)
Role
Founder; product and content design lead. Wrote the specs, prompts and behavior contracts. An engineer implemented them.
Stack
Next.js + Capacitor, Supabase, Google Places API, Anthropic API
Timeline
April–October 2026
01 — The idea

The best thing about a bar is rarely the thing everyone agrees on.

I was testing Sauntr in Savannah when I hit a review that mentioned a themed cookout the regulars run upstairs, a TV-show in-joke. One line, and I knew more about the place than its 4.5 stars or the platform's summary could tell me. That became the feature: Samuel finds the one detail a friend would mention and puts it on the card as "Samuel Sauntr Says."

02 — The prompt and its contract

The agent was allowed, and expected, to decline.

Planned in the April 26 sprint plan as one sentence per venue card, and locked on May 28. One Claude call per venue on first load, cached in Supabase, so cost grows with venues rather than users.

Locked prompt · May 28, 2026
"Analyze the reviews and summarize them into one or two sentences that would make a person feel like they got a special insight from a close friend about the venue. If the reviews don't contain enough specific or unexpected detail to write a meaningful insight, respond with only the word NONE."
Rule 1Use the prompt exactly as written. It was tuned for the "close friend" register.
Rule 2max_tokens: 200
Rule 3When the model returns NONE, hide Samuel entirely. No filler like "a local favorite."

Blank is better than generic. The NONE path was a design decision from the start, not a fallback added later.

03 — Implementation audit · July 3

It worked. It also wasn't the version we'd locked.

On venues nobody curated, the engine produced exactly the close-friend register, such as a bar where one bartender cooks and serves, with a specific sleeper menu item and its price. The code audit also found that production was running an earlier prompt.

04 — The incident · closed beta

Then Samuel accused a real bar of mistreating its customers.

The line was specific, and it sounded like the close friend I'd asked for. It was also exactly the wrong kind of specific: a factual claim about how a real business treats people, in Samuel's voice. I've left the venue's name off, because putting the claim next to it would just publish it again. 05 — Mitigation design

Defense in depth, so no single layer has to be perfect.

"Always stay positive" isn't enough: a false positive fact ("staff know every regular by name") is just as unverifiable. The rules separate impression from assertion, and make silence the safe answer.

Specced · never shipped
Layer 1

Generation prompt constraints

  1. Negative-claim exclusions. No allegations of staff misconduct, mistreatment, discrimination, safety, health, cleanliness or illegality; no negative factual claims about identifiable people.
  2. Opinion, not fact. Insights stay in impression language ("has the feel of a place where regulars linger").
  3. NONE as a safety control, not just a quality control. When in doubt, the agent says nothing.
  4. Ground truth. Every claim supported by the source reviews; no made-up quantifiers ("multiple people," "many say").
Layer 2

Independent screening agent

  • A separate, cheaper model (Haiku) with one narrow job: does this assert a negative factual claim about staff, conduct, safety, health or legality? PASS or BLOCK. A narrow classification is far more reliable than asking a creative generator to police itself.
  • Fail closed. On BLOCK, return NONE. Never auto-rewrite: a rewrite adds cost and can introduce new errors.
  • Placed before the cache write, so the cache only ever holds vetted output. Runs once per venue on a miss: fractions of a cent, no added delay on cached venues.
Layer 3

Operations

  • A notice-and-takedown path so venues can flag and remove a summary.
  • Media/tech E&O insurance before scaling users.

These were specced and added to the sprint tracker. They never shipped, because the next finding retired the feature first.

06 — The decision

It was working. I retired it anyway.

While I was speccing the fix, I found the bigger problem: the feature's whole mechanic was the thing it wasn't allowed to do. The data provider's terms don't allow new content built from its reviews, and that's all the feature did. Switching review sources wouldn't have touched the defamation risk either, because that comes from writing claims about businesses at all. So I didn't patch it. I gave Samuel a different job.

Why not patch it

The mechanic was the violation

Breaking the provider's terms risked losing API access, which would break the whole app. Another major review API explicitly bans feeding its content into generative AI.

07 — What replaced it

A behavior contract enforced in code, not by convention.

Three successive artifacts. The Voice Spec v1.0 (May): "a sommelier, not a salesperson," two sentences max, no questions or calls to action, and the Seen Test on every line. Samuel Says v1 (October): the contract written as a subagent. Content Framework v2 (October): surfaces, signals and a claims ladder.

08 — Transfer

The same patterns, in healthcare conversational AI.

The objects change. The discipline doesn't: decide what the agent may know, what it may claim, and when silence or a handoff is the right answer.

Sauntr patternHealthcare equivalent
Negative factual claims about a named businessClinical claims about a patient: diagnosis, dosing, prognosis
Opinion vs. fact registerSupportive and educational language vs. clinical assertion
NONE / silence as a first-class outputDecline, defer, or escalate to a clinician
Deterministic gate before the modelTriage rules and red-flag detection before generation
Narrow second-agent screenerAn independent safety classifier rather than self-policing
Fail closed, never auto-rewriteOn a safety flag, use the safe fallback rather than "fixing" the output
Ground truth, no made-up quantifiersEvery claim traceable to the source record or approved guidance
Claims ladder tied to evidenceConfidence-gated language; no certainty the data can't support
Type-level boundary on model inputsData minimization; PHI scoped by contract, not convention
Vet before the cache writeNothing unvetted persisted or reused
Verification over "marked complete"Safety controls proven with evals and tests, not assumed from a ticket
Retire rather than patchSome use cases can't be made safe and get scoped out
09 — What I took from it

The model didn't misbehave. It did what I asked, which was to find the most specific thing in the reviews, and sometimes the most specific thing is an accusation. What I'd tell another team: decide what the agent is allowed to know before you decide how it sounds. Treat silence as an answer. And when the mechanism itself is the risk, don't patch it. Retire it.
